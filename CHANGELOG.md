# Changelog

Items marked with ⚠️ are breaking changes.

## v0.1.0 - 2026-11-22

The first release of fushimi, a fork of inari v2.0.0; the entries below are the changes since inari v2.0.0.

### Added

- Added support for architectures other than x86-64 and AArch64, such as wasm32 and riscv64, with a portable backend that does not change the rounding mode.
- Added 32-bit x86 Linux (`i686-unknown-linux-gnu`) and x86-64 Windows with the MSVC toolchain to the targets that CI tests. CI runs the unit and integration tests there, but not those that compare with MPFR, which are compiled only on 64-bit Linux and macOS.
- Added AArch64 Linux (`aarch64-unknown-linux-gnu`) to the targets that CI tests, under QEMU's user-mode emulation. CI runs the unit tests there, among them those that compare with MPFR, those that compare the AArch64 backend with the portable one, and those that compare the portable one with operations that round with the rounding mode switched in FPCR.
- Added the Cargo feature `force-generic`, which uses the portable backend on x86-64 and AArch64 too, and on x86-64 keeps the multi-precision kernel from detecting the CPU at run time.
- Added a backend for WebAssembly that uses 128-bit SIMD instructions, enabled by the target feature `simd128`. It makes most interval operations other than multiplication 1.6 to 1.9 times as fast as the portable backend, and multiplication 1.3 to 1.5 times as fast.
- Added a backend for x86-64 processors that have SSE4.2 but neither AVX nor FMA, which lowers the lowest supported processor from Haswell to Nehalem. Like the WebAssembly backend, it does not change the rounding mode. On such a processor, it makes most interval operations 1.6 to 2.5 times as fast as the portable backend.
- Added correctly rounded `f64` functions in the module `fushimi::cr`: `exp`, `exp2`, `exp10`, `ln`, `log2`, `log10`, `sin`, `cos`, `tan`, `asin`, `acos`, `atan`, `atan2`, `sinh`, `cosh`, `tanh`, `asinh`, `acosh`, `atanh`, `pow` and `powi`, each rounded to nearest with ties to even, toward -∞ and toward +∞, such as `cr::exp_rn`, `cr::exp_rd` and `cr::exp_ru`. They give MPFR's values at the zeros, at the infinities and outside the domains, and `pow` gives those of C99 and MPFR at a finite x < 0 and at x = -∞. Each settles some arguments by rules, and the others by Ziv's strategy with enclosures of up to 4,096 bits. At an argument that reaches that cap with no rounding test passing, a function returns the rounding of a bound of the last enclosure, which is not known to be correctly rounded, but none of the 9,949,560 hard-to-round arguments that CORE-MATH lists for these functions reaches it. Tests on 64-bit Linux and macOS compare them with MPFR in all three roundings, at a sample of those arguments among others.
- Added correctly rounded `cbrt`, `hypot` and `sqrt` to `fushimi::cr`, each rounded to nearest with ties to even, toward -∞ and toward +∞, such as `cr::cbrt_rn`, `cr::hypot_rd` and `cr::sqrt_ru`. `cbrt` and `hypot` first try a fast path in `f64`, proved in Rocq to return the result rounded wherever it returns one, and only where it does not decide the rounding compute an exact integer cube or square root with the kernel, without Ziv's strategy and so with no cap. They give MPFR's values at the zeros and the infinities, including `hypot(±∞, y) = +∞` for a NaN `y`. `sqrt_rn` is `f64::sqrt`, and `sqrt_rd` and `sqrt_ru` are those of the backend of the interval arithmetic. Tests on 64-bit Linux and macOS compare them with MPFR in all three roundings, and `cbrt` and `hypot` at a sample of the hard-to-round arguments that CORE-MATH lists for them. On x uniform in [-10, 10), and on x of a random sign with a uniformly random exponent from -1074 to 1023 and significand, a call of `cbrt` of `cr` takes 1.4 to 1.5 times as many instructions as the correctly rounded `cbrt` of CORE-MATH in the same rounding, as counted with cachegrind in fushimi's own test binary built for Haswell. A call of `hypot` takes 1.4 to 1.5 times as many as that of CORE-MATH on x and y uniform in [-10, 10), and 1.9 to 2.2 times as many on x and y of random signs with uniformly random exponents from -1074 to 1023 and significands, drawn again until their binary exponents differ by 27 or more, where it computes a square root only for a few pairs with a subnormal argument. Where the rounding test of the fast path toward -∞ or +∞ fails, as it does at an exact result (`cr::cbrt_rd(8.0)`, `cr::hypot_ru(3.0, 4.0)`), the fast path checks in integers whether the `f64` nearest its approximation is the exact result, and returns it if it is: in a crate that links `fushimi::cr`, built for Haswell, as counted with cachegrind, on exact cubes and on scaled Pythagorean pairs, rounded toward -∞ or +∞, a call of `cbrt` takes 0.90 times as many instructions as that of CORE-MATH, and one of `hypot` 0.94 to 0.95 times as many. Arguments that the fast path declines, such as results of `hypot` midway between two `f64`s rounded to nearest and `hypot` of two subnormals, still take the kernel's path.
- Added `Interval::cbrt`, `Interval::hypot`, `DecInterval::cbrt` and `DecInterval::hypot`, IEEE 1788's recommended functions rootn(x, 3) and hypot(x, y), which compute their bounds with the functions of the kernel that `cr::cbrt_rd`, `cr::cbrt_ru`, `cr::hypot_rd` and `cr::hypot_ru` call, and return the tightest interval at every argument. `hypot` of a bounded box can overflow, as in `hypot([f64::MAX, f64::MAX], [f64::MAX, f64::MAX])` = [`f64::MAX`, +∞], which `DecInterval::hypot` decorates `dac`, as IEEE 1788 asks.
- Added the module `fushimi::simd`, with the tokens `Baseline`, `V2`, `V3` and `V4`, the traits `Simd` and `WithLevel` and the function `dispatch`, and the methods `add_with`, `sub_with`, `mul_with`, `div_with`, `mul_add_with`, `recip_with`, `sqr_with` and `sqrt_with` of `Interval` and `DecInterval`, which take a token, so that interval arithmetic can run at a level of the CPU detected at run time. `V2`, `V3` and `V4` exist only on x86-64, where `V2::try_new`, `V3::try_new` and `V4::try_new` return a token only on a CPU that has the target features of the token, and `V4` only with the feature `avx512`, which needs Rust 1.89 or later; without it, `dispatch` runs at most at `V3`. In a build for `x86-64` without the feature `force-generic`, whose operations without a token use the portable backend, the operations at `V2` run the `sse42` backend, those at `V3` the `fma` backend, in which the slow paths that it takes from the portable backend run without the FMA, as they do in the portable backend, and those at `V4` the `avx512f` backend, which those at `V4` also run in every other build that has neither `force-generic` nor the target feature `avx512f`, such as one for `x86-64-v3`. Every token gives the same intervals and decorations; only the speed differs.

### Changed

- ⚠️ Renamed the crate to `fushimi`, a fork of inari by Masashi Mizuno: `use inari::` becomes `use fushimi::`, and a dependency that keeps the name `inari` needs `package = "fushimi"`. `--cfg inari_force_no_fma` becomes `--cfg fushimi_force_no_fma`; the old one now does nothing, and rustc accepts it without a warning. The logical path of the Rocq proofs, `Inari`, becomes `Fushimi`.
- ⚠️ Bumped the minimum supported Rust version to 1.85.0.
- Made the default backend for x86-64 processors with AVX and FMA compute the directed roundings explicitly, instead of switching the rounding mode around every instruction. No backend for x86-64 changes the rounding mode any more. On a Zen 2 processor, interval addition and subtraction take 0.47 times as long as before, multiplication 0.49 to 0.62 (0.78 to 0.79 where the products are below 2^-968), division 0.67 to 0.69, evaluating a degree-8 polynomial 0.41 to 0.45, and a 3×3 determinant 0.33. Only the latency of a chain of dependent `add_ru`, `div_ru` and `mul_add_ru` operations became slightly worse, by 3 to 12 percent.
- Made the AArch64 backend compute the directed roundings with error-free transformations and the fused multiply-add, as the default backend for x86-64 does, instead of switching the rounding mode in FPCR around every operation, with the same results as before. No backend changes the rounding mode any more. On an Apple M1 Pro, nearly all of the time of these operations had gone to the writes to FPCR, as a prototype of this backend showed.
- Made `(Dec)Interval::sqrt` about 2.5 times faster on x86-64 processors without AVX-512F, by computing the square root without changing the rounding mode.

- Made interval literals parse without GMP and MPFR, with a big integer of fushimi's own. That the magnitude of a number is above 2^1024 or below 2^-1074, the smallest positive `f64`, is now told from the numbers of bits of its numerator and denominator and from its exponents where these suffice, without computing its power of ten. On a Zen 2 processor, `[1e1900000000]` and `[1e-2147483648]`, whose powers of ten have over 6 × 10^9 bits, parse in less than a microsecond, where GMP and MPFR took over 20 s and 2 GB of memory. ### Removed

- ⚠️ Removed the Cargo feature `gmp`, which was enabled by default, and the features `gmp-mpfr-sys`, `nom` and `rug`, which Cargo derived from the optional dependencies that `gmp` enabled. Literals, formatting and the elementary functions need neither GMP nor MPFR now (see above), and `nom` is a dependency of every build.
- ⚠️ Removed the Cargo feature `libm`, which Cargo derived from the optional dependency `libm`, and which only the examples `exp` and `log` used. They now take `libm` as a dev-dependency.

### Fixed

- Fixed `(Dec)Interval::rad` to return `0.0` instead of `-0.0` for the interval [0, 0] when its upper bound is `-0.0`, as in `const_interval!(0.0, -0.0)`. It returned `-0.0` with Rust 1.96 on x86-64, because `f64::max` may return either zero when given `0.0` and `-0.0`.
- Fixed `(Dec)Interval::cancel_minus` and `(Dec)Interval::cancel_plus` to return the tightest interval instead of `(Dec)Interval::ENTIRE` for some arguments whose widths round to the same float and that have a bound of `f64::MIN` or `f64::MAX`, such as `x.cancel_minus(x)` for `x` = [`f64::MIN`, -3 × 2^970], which is [0, 0]. An intermediate result of the error-free sum that compares the widths overflowed.
- Fixed `(Dec)Interval::cos`, `(Dec)Interval::sin` and `(Dec)Interval::tan` to return the tightest interval, and `DecInterval::tan` the strongest decoration, as the documentation states, also where a bound of the argument is close to a multiple of π/2. They located the multiples of π/2 in the argument by dividing it by `Interval::PI` or `Interval::FRAC_PI_2`, which only encloses the quotient, so that a bound close to such a multiple, relative to its magnitude, could give a wider result. For example, `tan` of [7.853981633974484, 7.853981633974485], just above the pole 5π/2, was [-∞, +∞], decorated `trv`, and is now [-1718163698371049, -680181816043107], decorated `com`. Where the division cannot tell, they now find the multiples from the signs of the sine and the cosine of the bounds, which the multi-precision kernel (see above) rounds correctly wherever one of its rules or a rounding test of Ziv's strategy settles them.
- Fixed `{:.0x}` of `(Dec)Interval`s to print the exact digit of a positive upper bound or a negative lower bound of at most 4 significant bits, which MPFR printed one too large, as in `[0x8p-3,0x9p-3]` for [1, 1], or, where that made 16, as `0x` alone, as in `[0xfp+0,0x]` for [15, 15]. They are now `[0x8p-3,0x8p-3]` and `[0xfp+0,0xfp+0]`.
